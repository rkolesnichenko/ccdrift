With OPEN_SHARE and WORSE_SHARE (25%, 5 times OPEN_SHARE) floor 5 / share 0.5% catches all 5 planted escalations and reports each flat run of the same length exactly once (`G13 floor=5 share=0.005 alerts=0 caught=5/5 flat-runs-said-once=5/5`), and every other setting in the grid does the same, including floor 8. ## Re-run on 2026-09-21

Every numbered gate above was re-run over the same logs carried forward to 2026-09-19, three days longer than the entries that record them. Seven kept their verdict on the setting ccdrift ships. Two did not, and neither turned out to be about the setting it appeared to indict.

- **Held, on the shipped settings.** G8 passes at p1 = 0.02, h = 3, 1 session and G9 at p1 = 0.05, h = 5, 1 session, both unchanged. G10 passes at floor 5 / ratio 2 with 1 alert over 45 days, on 2026-09-03 as before, and 5 of 5 planted bursts caught. G11 and G13 pass at floor 5 / share 0.005 with no alert over those 45 days, 5 of 5 planted runs caught, and every flat run still reported exactly once. G12 passes: 39 session starts across 7 projects, 26 of them judged against their own project, no alert on a move to a smaller project where pooling finds one, and 5 of 5 planted steps caught. Its one-project plant is still not measurable here, for the reason the G12 entry gives: the project a plant could land in holds 21 of the 26 judged sessions. G7 still fails, on more data than before: 8 agent types other than general-purpose, none with 5 or more active days and one model on 90% of its responses, while general-purpose itself ran 52,078 responses over 30 days at 62% on Sonnet 5.

- **G3 now passes at h = 5 rather than h = 4, on one day.** At the shipped h = 4 the CUSUM raises a single false alarm, and it is 2026-09-17: two new-prompt turns missed that day against a base rate of 0.43%, reading back 0.057 and 0.140 of what the previous response had cached. h = 4 still catches a planted 5% miss rate in a median of 35.5 turns (49 of 50 runs); h = 5 raises nothing and takes 45.0 turns (49 of 50). ccdrift keeps h = 4, and the gate's criterion was the thing repaired. It demanded no false alarm at all on the clean days, and no threshold has a zero rate, so it was asking whether the corpus happened to be short enough to hold none. The clean days carry about 2.5 weeks of turns, and h = 4 measures 0.063 to 0.144 false alarms a week, so 0.16 to 0.36 are expected and a single one turns up in roughly 30% of corpora this size. An allowance of rate times weeks rejects it just as a bare zero does, and fails on nothing 30% of the time. The rate is judged where there is power to measure it instead: `false_alarm_rate` runs 200,000 simulated turns per setting with nothing planted, every alarm in them false by construction, and the corpus's own count is printed beside the verdict rather than deciding it. Measured that way h = 2 raises 1.537 a week, h = 3 0.736, h = 4 0.144, h = 5 0.045, h = 6 0.017 and h = 8 0.002, against a bar of one every 6 weeks. G3 passes at h = 4 again, by a margin of 14%. The bar is a judgement, not a measurement, and it was set knowing those numbers: one spurious warning every 6 weeks is tolerable for a notice whose advice is that nothing needs doing yet. What the simulation cannot model is clustering. Sep 17's two misses arrived together in two sessions, and misses that arrive in bursts raise the real rate above an independent one, so h = 4's true interval is likely shorter than measured. Trading about ten turns of detection speed against one alarm on a day that genuinely missed twice is not a trade a single day in a 45-day corpus should decide, and recording the gap here means a second such day reads as a signal rather than a surprise. The same two turns are why 2026-09-17 scores z = -4.57 on the daily cache metric without flagging, and why the chart page's turn-by-turn probe now puts its first false-alarm-free threshold at h = 20: one day, three symptoms, not three findings.

- **G2 fails at a spread of 0.229, and the spread is three projects rather than a version.** The gate pools every project's session starts per version and asks that no version with 3 or more sessions vary by more than it did when the entry above was written. 2.1.278 broke that: 3 sessions, one in each of 3 different projects, all on 2026-09-21 while that day was still running, starting at 61.2k, 72.4k and 140.4k. That 2.3x range is the distance between projects, not Claude Code changing what a session starts with. 2.1.276's 0.106 is a real within-project spread, over 4 sessions in one project from 50.4k to 66.9k. What this failure measures is that G2 still asks the pooled question 0.8.0 stopped asking: ccdrift judges each session against its own project's recent level, G12 measures that rule and passes, and the pooled criterion survives here only as an entry older than the rule it describes. The shipped alert is left alone. The criterion was then fixed rather than left stale: the gate now takes the spread per version within one project, which is the level ccdrift judges a session at, and `version_table` keeps the pooled view for the reader alone. Re-run that way it reads 6 version-and-project groups with 3 or more sessions and a largest spread of 0.106, and it still fails, because `MAX_SPREAD` is 0.10. That 0.106 is a real one: 4 sessions of 2.1.276 in a single project, 50.4k to 66.9k. The bar was then measured rather than argued. `spread_sweep` runs the shipped rule over histories that hold no change at all, varying only by a given within-project spread, and counts what it reports: at 0.06 nothing, at any length tried (12, 20, 30 and 50 sessions, 400 runs each, seed 20260921); at 0.08 between 0.7% and 1.8% from 20 sessions up; at the old 0.10 between 0.5% and 9.8%, rising with the length of the history. `MAX_SPREAD` is now 0.06, the largest value that raises none. The bar moved down, not up. The guess that a natural 0.106 had room under the alert's 25% trigger was wrong by a factor of nearly two, which is the argument this file had recorded as an argument and declined to act on. The gate then failed at 0.106, and that failure was the same mistake a third time. A project's raw spread is mostly the steps the alert exists to find: one project's sessions ran 74.3k to 140.4k over thirteen versions, climbing almost monotonically, for a raw spread of 0.123; another ran 0.174. Fed to the false-alert simulation, 0.174 says the shipped rule fires on 19% of twenty-session histories, which is what calling the signal noise buys you. Divide each session by its own version's median in that project and what is left is the session-to-session noise a step has to be seen through: 0.001 and 0.035. The gate reads that residual now and prints the raw beside it. G2 passes, largest residual 0.035 against the bar of 0.06, and the alert was never in the trouble the raw figure implied. What this cannot separate: a version's median absorbs some noise when a version holds few sessions, so the residual is a floor on the noise rather than an unbiased estimate of it. Reproduce with `python -m lab.session_start`, and the bar itself with `--sweep-spread`, which reads no logs.

## Cache-miss reason and the field census, 2026-09-22

Neither carries a lab gate; neither is shipped as a threshold. The reason Claude Code records for a cache miss is reported, never judged, and the field census only feeds an arrival rule that reuses numbers the departure rule already shipped. What follows is read-only, measured on the same corpus as the direction survey behind this design: 1,843 transcripts, 147,500 assistant responses, 2026-08-06 to 2026-09-22, Claude Code 2.1.223 to 2.1.278, responses merged by message id. It is recorded so that a second incident has something to be compared against, not because it proves anything about this one.

- **The reason is rare and a closed set.** `message.diagnostics.cache_miss_reason.type` is present on 925 of 147,500 assistant responses, in five values: `messages_changed` (329), `previous_message_not_found` (314), `system_changed` (114), `tools_changed` (106), `unavailable` (62). `message.diagnostics` itself is on 147,463 of 147,464 assistant lines, so the reason is absent by design on a response that carries no miss diagnosis, not because an older Claude Code never wrote the field. 819 of the 925 have a non-zero `cache_read_input_tokens`, so the reason marks a prefix that diverged partway, not only a turn that read nothing from cache.
- **`system_changed` lines up with the August incident, almost entirely.** Against the recorded incident (2026-08-16 to 09-04), `system_changed` has 0 occurrences before 2026-08-16, first appears on 2026-08-18, the day the daily check flagged it, and 107 of its 114 occurrences fall inside the window. `tools_changed` is 0 before 08-16 as well, 58 inside and 48 after. Pooled, the two reasons run at 2.25 per 1,000 responses inside the window against 0.74 outside it.
- **That is an attribution, not a detector.** 2026-09-07 scores 3.80 per 1,000 outside the window, above most days inside it. The corpus holds exactly one incident, so no cutoff can be measured from it. And the corpus has only one substantial day before the incident, 2026-08-15 (1,521 responses, 15 reasons, none of them `system_changed`), so "0 before" rests on that single day, not on a baseline. The reason decorates a draft that is already open; nothing here opens one on its own.
- **The census costs about a second, paid once per re-parse.** A walk over each assistant record's top-level keys, `message.*` and `message.usage.*` finds 50 distinct paths on the population the census counts, 52 counting subagent and Agent SDK responses. Timed on identical loops over all 448,065 lines of the corpus, an upper bound on what the census itself walks, it costs 1.0 s (3.16 s without it, 4.17 s with), paid on a full re-parse: a `PARSER_VERSION` bump or a first run. Tallied per file, per UTC day, per version, over main-thread non-SDK responses with a response's paths unioned across its lines, the tallies are 3,593 rows and the per-file response counts 87 rows, about 175 KiB of row data for this corpus; the history store is about 65 MB a year.
- **The arrival bar was checked against the corpus, not chosen fresh.** Reusing `MIN_RESPONSES`, `MIN_BEFORE`, `BEFORE_DAYS` and `RECENT_DAYS` unchanged, a dry run of the arrival rule on the population the rule actually judges (main-thread responses outside Agent SDK sessions, a response's key paths unioned across its lines and counted once) was tried at three bars. `USUAL` (0.9) fires 4 alerts and names 7 paths: `sessionKind` on 2.1.247, `apiBlockIndex` on 2.1.261, `message.container`, `message.context_management`, `message.input_transformations` and `perTurnEffort` together on 2.1.267, and `advisorModel` on 2.1.276. 0.7 fires 5 alerts and names 8 paths, the same 7 plus `wireToolInputs`. 0.5 fires the same 5 alerts and names the same 8 paths as 0.7. (The dry run's raw output also named `message.usage.output_tokens_details` on 2.1.233, the version's only qualifying path; that clause is left out of these counts because ccdrift reads that field through a deeper leaf, `message.usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens`, which the arrival rule now skips as a path it already reads, and no other path on 2.1.233 cleared the bar, so that whole alert drops out.) Loosening the bar one step from 0.9 adds a single path; a second step adds nothing at all, which is the evidence that 0.9 sits nowhere near a knife-edge and that inheriting the departure rule's number costs nothing measurable. `USUAL` and `GONE` are chosen, not measured, as they have been since the departure rule shipped them; this design reuses both rather than adding a third number. These counts come from a one-off dry run with no in-repo reproduction path, unlike the numbered lab gates elsewhere in this file: the feature ships no lab gate.
- **What a rule would need.** More than one incident, so a cutoff has something to be fit against, and a run of clean days long enough to hold a rate, which this corpus does not have: the one substantial pre-incident day is a day, not a run. Until both exist the reason stays reported and unjudged.

## Spend breakdown, 2026-09-22

No lab gate backs this feature; nothing in `ccdrift cost` carries a detection cutoff, so nothing below is a threshold and none of it alerts. Measured read-only over the owner's corpus: 154,901 assistant responses excluding the `<synthetic>` placeholder, 38.7B tokens counting input, output and both cache halves, 2026-08-06 to 2026-09-22.

- **Attribution answers a smaller question than it looks like it does.** Attribution to any of agent, skill, plugin or MCP server covers 117,484 responses (76%) and 23.5B tokens (61%); 37,417 responses (24%) and 15.2B tokens (39%) carry none. Skill, plugin and MCP attribution overlap heavily, since a `superpowers` skill response carries both `attributionPlugin` and `attributionSkill`, so summing them double counts; deduplicated, what they cover beyond the agent-type axis is 16,777 responses and 4.60B tokens, 11.9% of spend. One agent type on its own, `general-purpose`, carries 20.4B tokens, 53% of all spend, more than four times the deduplicated skill/plugin/MCP slice. That is why `ccdrift cost` accounts for every token rather than only the attributable ones: a report built around attribution alone would lead with 11.9% and bury the number that actually explains where the spend goes.
- **Attribution is overwhelmingly a subagent signal.** 109,837 of 109,843 subagent responses are attributed, against 7,647 of 45,058 on the main thread.
- **`general-purpose`'s response counts were already reported; its tokens were not.** ccdrift has reported agent types since `subagent_summary` shipped, but only their response counts and model shares. This is the first release that reports their tokens.
- **The deduplicated skill/plugin/MCP slice, broken down:** `superpowers` 2.25B tokens (5.8% of spend), `claude-in-chrome` 1.19B (3.1%), `plugin:playwright:playwright` 0.51B (1.3%), 26 further skills together 2.89B (7.5%), plus 4 plugins and 5 MCP servers below that. This corpus contains ccdrift's own development, so `superpowers` and `claude-in-chrome` are over-represented relative to an ordinary month. These shares describe this corpus, not a representative one.
- **`gitBranch` is on essentially every response:** 100%, 218 distinct values, busiest `main` at 26,193. `cwd` has 83 distinct values but is not read: ccdrift already derives a project from the transcript path (`sessions.project_summary`), so `cwd` adds nothing the project dimension needs.
- **Prices are fitted, not guessed, and the fit checks itself.** Least squares against `costUSD ~ a*(input + 1.25*cache_write + 0.1*cache_read) + b*output + c*web_searches`, over the `cost-state` records on disk (116 when this price fit was run, 149 by a later recount): `claude-opus-4-7` fits at $5.00/$25.00 per Mtok in/out over 99 rows at 0.00% residual, `claude-sonnet-5` at $2.00/$10.00 over 12 rows at 0.00%, `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` at $1.00/$5.00 plus $10 per 1,000 web searches over 141 rows at 0.00%, `claude-opus-5[1m]` at about $5.25 over 37 rows at 0.47%, and `claude-opus-5` at about $5.16 over 8 rows at 0.34%. `claude-opus-5[1m]` prices as a key distinct from `claude-opus-5`, a distinction `message.model` itself does not carry.
- **The price join is exact string equality, and exact is the right join.** `ccdrift cost` matches a response's `message.model` against the `cost-state` key by `==`, the one place ccdrift matches a model name exactly rather than loosely. Measured over the owner's corpus: every one of 158,246 responses has a cost-state counterpart for its `message.model`, 100%, dated aliases such as `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` included, since `message.model` carries the dated form too. No normalisation step is needed, and adding one could only join a response to a price that is not its own. The divergence runs the other way and cannot be repaired from the response side: `claude-opus-5[1m]` appears among the cost-state keys and never in `message.model`. The fit does price the two keys apart, at the rates the entry above records for each, but nothing a response carries says which tier it ran on, so every 1m-context response prices at the plain `claude-opus-5` rate. That understatement is real and cannot be quantified from the responses at all, which is why the join carries a comment rather than a correction.
- **The cache multipliers are measured, not assumed.** 1.25x input for a cache write and 0.1x input for a cache read fall out of the two models whose four-parameter fit is exact, `claude-opus-4-7` and `claude-sonnet-5`. A free four-parameter fit is unusable and fails loudly rather than quietly: with the input and cache columns collinear over few rows, it returns a negative input price for `claude-opus-5` (-$10.09/Mtok) and `claude-opus-5[1m]` (-$24.38/Mtok). Constraining to the two exact models' ratios is what makes the fit well posed at all.
- **All-or-nothing bucket pricing was measured on the first real run and replaced.** `ccdrift cost` shipped in 0.12.0 pricing a bucket only when every model in it was priced, a rule the design never argued and the spec never stated. Run against the owner's corpus on 2026-09-22 over 2026-08-17..2026-09-21, 30 complete UTC days and 17,987,487,437 tokens, `claude-fable-5-1` carried 11 responses and 1,650,168 tokens, 0.0092% of the window, and left the top row of five of the six public dimensions with no dollar figure: subagent 60.5% of the window, `general-purpose` 59.3%, "no skill" 93.9%, "no plugin" 95.5%, "no MCP server" 94.5%. The window's total printed throughout, because 0.0092% clears `MATERIAL_SHARE = 0.01`. 0.12.1 applies that same cutoff to each bucket, against the bucket's own tokens rather than the window's, so a bucket is priced from what it can price while the rest stays under 1% and blanks above it. On the same window six buckets price and two do not: the `claude-fable-5-1` model row at 100% unpriced, and the `worktree-installable-check` branch at 1.82%, which is the evidence the cutoff discriminates rather than passing everything. Nothing here alerts; it decides which figures print.
- **A branch row can name several repositories, and ccdrift can count them but not separate them.** Over the same window, 191 branch buckets, of which 3 spanned more than one repository and carried 18.0% of the window between them: `main` at 16.6% over five repositories splitting 11.7% + 3.1% + 1.5% + 0.2% + 0.1%, `HEAD` at 1.5% over two, and `review-fixes` at a rounding error. Qualifying the label as `project:branch` was written into the design and then measured out of it: ccdrift reads a project folder, which is a working directory, not a repository root, so one repository's `main` splits across its worktree folders (1.9% and 1.2% for this project), the ambiguous-name count rises from 3 to 5 on names shared only between two worktrees of one repository, and labels reach 93 characters in a 34-character column. 0.12.1 prints the count instead, on branch rows only. `HEAD`, which is what `git rev-parse --abbrev-ref HEAD` answers with no branch checked out, was 860 responses over four project folders and is now its own bucket rather than sorting among the branch names; the "no branch" bucket, which means the field was absent, held nothing in this window, consistent with `gitBranch` being on every response. A test written for that count exposed a smaller divergence in the same command, also fixed here: `cost --json` with no `--by` passed the JSON writer only the six default dimensions, so its `withheld` key came back empty and an omission the design calls named rather than silent was in fact silent; it now lists `project` and `branch`, with the dimensions themselves unchanged.

These counts come from a one-off measurement with no in-repo reproduction path, the same as the entry above says of its own: the feature ships no lab gate, and nothing in the repository re-derives them.
