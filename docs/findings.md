# Findings so far

From one developer's Claude Code logs, Aug 6 - Sep 16, 2026. Aggregate numbers only.

- **Cache: a real signal.** A Claude Code caching regression (versions 2.1.233-2.1.258, in use Aug 16 - Sep 4) is flagged from Aug 18. Its first days score z = -3.8, -5.4, -2.8, -3.2 against a cutoff of 3.0, so a flag takes 3 deviant days among any 4: once Claude Code's 30-day transcript cleanup deleted part of Aug 15-16, requiring 3 in a row missed it. With the incident left out, a 5% drop is caught from all 3 starting days in the 12 clean days that remain.
- **Following an incident.** Replaying the daily check once for each day from Aug 17 to Sep 16, the run on Aug 22 flags the caching regression from Aug 18, on 2.1.235 and 2.1.233, with ~5.6M tokens re-cached so far. Judged against the 14 days before its first flagged day (Aug 6, 7, 15, 16, 17), every day of the regression from Aug 18 to Sep 1 scores z ≤ −3.1. Against a rolling baseline z was back to about 0 by Aug 26, while 5–10% of prompt turns still missed. Pooling 3 days, the run on Sep 7 finds it back to normal from Sep 4 and closes it with Sep 3 as its last day. That alert names 2.1.247 and 2.1.261: on the CLI main thread Sep 4 still ran 2.1.247, with no misses in 38 prompt turns, and Sep 5–6 ran 2.1.261 (2.1.259 appeared only in SDK sessions, 2.1.260 in 12 responses on Sep 4). Judging single days instead closed it on Sep 4 "from Sep 2", though Sep 3 still had a miss. From Aug 18 to Sep 3 missed prompt turns re-cached ~16M tokens beyond the usual miss rate; all misses from Aug 16 to Sep 4 wrote 17.6M. The replay sent no other alert: no Haiku flag, setting change or blank cache metric. A first check on Sep 16 with no state sends no alert, since the regression began more than 14 days earlier, and builds an 8.8 MB history.
- **Haiku: a real signal on the main thread**, which never uses Haiku, so a 5% shift is caught from every starting day. Subagent Haiku bursts make the all-turn share noisy: on 5 synthetic logs it was falsely flagged on 3 (on 1 when the deviant days had to be in a row), the main thread on none.
- **Effort: not detectable from one user's logs.** Over the 12 clean days its daily level swung between 0.33 and 0.67, more than a 70% cut in thinking tokens moves it, so no size of drop was caught, on all turns or the main thread. Detecting it points to pooled data from many users.
- **Effort with logged thinking tokens.** Claude Code logs a thinking token count on 52% of responses (11466 of 22181, leaving out Aug 16 – Sep 4); it tracks the signature estimate at r = 1.00. Over the clean days the daily effort level ranges 0.33–0.59 with logged counts against 0.33–0.67 with the estimate on all turns, and 0.24–0.64 against 0.27–0.74 on the main thread. With logged counts the effort sweep catches no drop from every start on all turns or the main thread, the same as with the estimate.
- **Turn latency: too noisy to watch.** 658 main-thread turn durations over 13 clean days, the last of them Sep 16 up to the run. The daily median turn takes 104.5 s (range 30.4–204.7, spread 0.48), and the daily flag rule catches no slowdown up to ×2 from every start (×2 from 2 of 4), with no clean days flagged without a planted slowdown. The logged `messageCount` can't normalise this: it counts the whole session so far, not the turn. On 1,685 main-thread turns it equals the user, assistant, attachment and system records logged before it in the session on 83%, and on 97% of 1,654 consecutive turns it grows by exactly the records added in between; its median is 2,473, while a turn holds a median of 12 user and assistant records. Duration per message (daily median 51 ms, range 14–94, spread 0.84) therefore falls as a session grows, whatever the speed, so it isn't a latency measure; it also catches no slowdown up to ×2.
- **Session-start context: steady enough to alert on.** 31 sessions; per version the median ranged from 44.3k to 318.0k, and every version with 3 or more sessions stayed within a spread of 0.038 (MAD over median). The alert rule found 2 steps: 104k -> 136k from 2026-08-27 and 128k -> 54k from 2026-09-10, both on a version new to their baseline. G2 passes, so ccdrift alerts on session-start changes.

- **Early warning on cache misses: passes, on thin evidence.** A Bernoulli likelihood-ratio CUSUM on new-prompt misses (usual rate against 5%) with h = 4 caught a planted 5% miss rate in a median of 26 new-prompt turns (38 of 40 runs). Its 0 false alarms cover only 2 complete clean days, Sep 14 and 15 (and Sep 16 up to the run), the only ones whose 14 days before their week held the harness's 100 new-prompt turns; with the check's 200, no complete clean day qualifies. It alarmed on the real regression at 2026-08-18 21:55 UTC, 4 days before the daily check's alert, only because the harness then falls back to the 59 new-prompt turns before Aug 16 (none missed) for the usual rate. G3 passes, so the check runs hourly. As shipped, the warning can't fire until the 14 days before the latest week hold 200 or more new-prompt turns, so it needs two busy weeks of history before the latest week, and it wouldn't have fired during the August regression: replayed hour by hour from Aug 16, the 14 days before each week held 16–59 new-prompt turns until the daily check flagged the regression on Aug 22, and the open incident held it back after that. Over Sep 4–16 (549 new-prompt turns, 2 misses) the CUSUM raises no alarm at a usual rate of 0.2–1%.

- **Subagent models: no built-in agent keeps one model steadily enough.** Of 7 agent types other than general-purpose — 4 of Claude Code's own (Plan, Explore, claude-code-guide, statusline-setup) and 3 other agent types — none had 5 or more days with 5+ responses and one model on 90% or more of them; the closest was Plan, with only 1 such day (100% on Opus 5), and Explore's busiest 2 days ran 56% on Sonnet 5. A caller can pick any subagent's model, so ccdrift only reports subagent models. G7 fails.
- **Early warning on tool-loop cache misses: passes on the main thread and in subagents.** A tool-loop turn (not opening with a prompt, not right after a compaction, within 5 minutes of the previous response of its transcript) misses when it reads less than half of what that response had cached: 10 of 10911 main-thread turns over 30 days and 104 of 48717 subagent turns over 26 days, from 2026-08-06 to 2026-09-16. A Bernoulli likelihood-ratio CUSUM against the miss rate of the 14 days before each week (at least 1,000 turns, held within 0.2% and half of p1) was tried with p1 of 1%, 2% and 5%, h from 2 to 8, and alarms counted only when their misses come from 1 or 2 sessions. On the main thread (G8), p1 = 0.02 and h = 3 with 1 session raised no false alarm over the 10 days judged outside Aug 16 – Sep 4 and caught a planted 2% miss rate in a median of 96 turns (50 of 50 runs). In subagents (G9), p1 = 0.05 and h = 4 with 1 session raised no false alarm over the 9 days judged outside Aug 16 – Sep 4 and caught a planted 2% miss rate in a median of 125.5 turns (50 of 50 runs). Counting only alarms with misses from 2 sessions slowed the catch to a median of 513 turns at best (46 of 50 runs). No regression of tool-loop caching is known in these logs, so no alarm could be checked against a real one, and the days judged are few. G8 and G9 pass, so ccdrift warns about both.
