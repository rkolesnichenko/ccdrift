## Re-run on 2026-09-21

Every numbered gate above was re-run over the same logs carried forward to 2026-09-19, three days longer than the entries that record them. Seven kept their verdict on the setting ccdrift ships. Two did not, and neither turned out to be about the setting it appeared to indict.

G12 passes: 39 session starts across 7 projects, 26 of them judged against their own project, no alert on a move to a smaller project where pooling finds one, and 5 of 5 planted steps caught. - **G3 now passes at h = 5 rather than h = 4, on one day.** At the shipped h = 4 the CUSUM raises a single false alarm, and it is 2026-09-17: two new-prompt turns missed that day against a base rate of 0.43%, reading back 0.057 and 0.140 of what the previous response had cached. h = 4 still catches a planted 5% miss rate in a median of 35.5 turns (49 of 50 runs); h = 5 raises nothing and takes 45.0 turns (49 of 50). ccdrift keeps h = 4, and the gate's criterion was the thing repaired. It demanded no false alarm at all on the clean days, and no threshold has a zero rate, so it was asking whether the corpus happened to be short enough to hold none. The clean days carry about 2.5 weeks of turns, and h = 4 measures 0.063 to 0.144 false alarms a week, so 0.16 to 0.36 are expected and a single one turns up in roughly 30% of corpora this size. An allowance of rate times weeks rejects it just as a bare zero does, and fails on nothing 30% of the time. The rate is judged where there is power to measure it instead: `false_alarm_rate` runs 200,000 simulated turns per setting with nothing planted, every alarm in them false by construction, and the corpus's own count is printed beside the verdict rather than deciding it. Measured that way h = 2 raises 1.537 a week, h = 3 0.736, h = 4 0.144, h = 5 0.045, h = 6 0.017 and h = 8 0.002, against a bar of one every 6 weeks. G3 passes at h = 4 again, by a margin of 14%. The bar is a judgement, not a measurement, and it was set knowing those numbers: one spurious warning every 6 weeks is tolerable for a notice whose advice is that nothing needs doing yet. What the simulation cannot model is clustering. Sep 17's two misses arrived together in two sessions, and misses that arrive in bursts raise the real rate above an independent one, so h = 4's true interval is likely shorter than measured. Trading about ten turns of detection speed against one alarm on a day that genuinely missed twice is not a trade a single day in a 45-day corpus should decide, and recording the gap here means a second such day reads as a signal rather than a surprise. The same two turns are why 2026-09-17 scores z = -4.57 on the daily cache metric without flagging, and why the chart page's turn-by-turn probe now puts its first false-alarm-free threshold at h = 20: one day, three symptoms, not three findings.

## Cache-miss reason and the field census, 2026-09-22

Neither carries a lab gate; neither is shipped as a threshold. The reason Claude Code records for a cache miss is reported, never judged, and the field census only feeds an arrival rule that reuses numbers the departure rule already shipped. What follows is read-only, measured on the same corpus as the direction survey behind this design: 1,843 transcripts, 147,500 assistant responses, 2026-08-06 to 2026-09-22, Claude Code 2.1.223 to 2.1.278, responses merged by message id. It is recorded so that a second incident has something to be compared against, not because it proves anything about this one.

- **The reason is rare and a closed set.** `message.diagnostics.cache_miss_reason.type` is present on 925 of 147,500 assistant responses, in five values: `messages_changed` (329), `previous_message_not_found` (314), `system_changed` (114), `tools_changed` (106), `unavailable` (62). `message.diagnostics` itself is on 147,463 of 147,464 assistant lines, so the reason is absent by design on a response that carries no miss diagnosis, not because an older Claude Code never wrote the field. 819 of the 925 have a non-zero `cache_read_input_tokens`, so the reason marks a prefix that diverged partway, not only a turn that read nothing from cache.
- **`system_changed` lines up with the August incident, almost entirely.** Against the recorded incident (2026-08-16 to 09-04), `system_changed` has 0 occurrences before 2026-08-16, first appears on 2026-08-18, the day the daily check flagged it, and 107 of its 114 occurrences fall inside the window. `tools_changed` is 0 before 08-16 as well, 58 inside and 48 after. Pooled, the two reasons run at 2.25 per 1,000 responses inside the window against 0.74 outside it.
- **That is an attribution, not a detector.** 2026-09-07 scores 3.80 per 1,000 outside the window, above most days inside it. The corpus holds exactly one incident, so no cutoff can be measured from it. And the corpus has only one substantial day before the incident, 2026-08-15 (1,521 responses, 15 reasons, none of them `system_changed`), so "0 before" rests on that single day, not on a baseline. The reason decorates a draft that is already open; nothing here opens one on its own.
- **The census costs about a second, paid once per re-parse.** A walk over each assistant record's top-level keys, `message.*` and `message.usage.*` finds 50 distinct paths on the population the census counts, 52 counting subagent and Agent SDK responses. Timed on identical loops over all 448,065 lines of the corpus, an upper bound on what the census itself walks, it costs 1.0 s (3.16 s without it, 4.17 s with), paid on a full re-parse: a `PARSER_VERSION` bump or a first run. Tallied per file, per UTC day, per version, over main-thread non-SDK responses with a response's paths unioned across its lines, the tallies are 3,593 rows and the per-file response counts 87 rows, about 175 KiB of row data for this corpus; the history store is about 65 MB a year.
- **The arrival bar was checked against the corpus, not chosen fresh.** Reusing `MIN_RESPONSES`, `MIN_BEFORE`, `BEFORE_DAYS` and `RECENT_DAYS` unchanged, a dry run of the arrival rule on the population the rule actually judges (main-thread responses outside Agent SDK sessions, a response's key paths unioned across its lines and counted once) was tried at three bars. `USUAL` (0.9) fires 4 alerts and names 7 paths: `sessionKind` on 2.1.247, `apiBlockIndex` on 2.1.261, `message.container`, `message.context_management`, `message.input_transformations` and `perTurnEffort` together on 2.1.267, and `advisorModel` on 2.1.276. 0.7 fires 5 alerts and names 8 paths, the same 7 plus `wireToolInputs`. 0.5 fires the same 5 alerts and names the same 8 paths as 0.7. (The dry run's raw output also named `message.usage.output_tokens_details` on 2.1.233, the version's only qualifying path; that clause is left out of these counts because ccdrift reads that field through a deeper leaf, `message.usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens`, which the arrival rule now skips as a path it already reads, and no other path on 2.1.233 cleared the bar, so that whole alert drops out.) Loosening the bar one step from 0.9 adds a single path; a second step adds nothing at all, which is the evidence that 0.9 sits nowhere near a knife-edge and that inheriting the departure rule's number costs nothing measurable. `USUAL` and `GONE` are chosen, not measured, as they have been since the departure rule shipped them; this design reuses both rather than adding a third number. These counts come from a one-off dry run with no in-repo reproduction path, unlike the numbered lab gates elsewhere in this file: the feature ships no lab gate.
- **What a rule would need.** More than one incident, so a cutoff has something to be fit against, and a run of clean days long enough to hold a rate, which this corpus does not have: the one substantial pre-incident day is a day, not a run. Until both exist the reason stays reported and unjudged.

## Spend breakdown, 2026-09-22

No lab gate backs this feature; nothing in `ccdrift cost` carries a detection cutoff, so nothing below is a threshold and none of it alerts. Measured read-only over the owner's corpus: 154,901 assistant responses excluding the `<synthetic>` placeholder, 38.7B tokens counting input, output and both cache halves, 2026-08-06 to 2026-09-22.

- **Attribution answers a smaller question than it looks like it does.** Attribution to any of agent, skill, plugin or MCP server covers 117,484 responses (76%) and 23.5B tokens (61%); 37,417 responses (24%) and 15.2B tokens (39%) carry none. Skill, plugin and MCP attribution overlap heavily, since a `superpowers` skill response carries both `attributionPlugin` and `attributionSkill`, so summing them double counts; deduplicated, what they cover beyond the agent-type axis is 16,777 responses and 4.60B tokens, 11.9% of spend. One agent type on its own, `general-purpose`, carries 20.4B tokens, 53% of all spend, more than four times the deduplicated skill/plugin/MCP slice. That is why `ccdrift cost` accounts for every token rather than only the attributable ones: a report built around attribution alone would lead with 11.9% and bury the number that actually explains where the spend goes.
- **Attribution is overwhelmingly a subagent signal.** 109,837 of 109,843 subagent responses are attributed, against 7,647 of 45,058 on the main thread.
- **`general-purpose`'s response counts were already reported; its tokens were not.** ccdrift has reported agent types since `subagent_summary` shipped, but only their response counts and model shares. This is the first release that reports their tokens.
- **The deduplicated skill/plugin/MCP slice, broken down:** `superpowers` 2.25B tokens (5.8% of spend), `claude-in-chrome` 1.19B (3.1%), `plugin:playwright:playwright` 0.51B (1.3%), 26 further skills together 2.89B (7.5%), plus 4 plugins and 5 MCP servers below that. This corpus contains ccdrift's own development, so `superpowers` and `claude-in-chrome` are over-represented relative to an ordinary month. These shares describe this corpus, not a representative one.
- **`gitBranch` is on essentially every response:** 100%, 218 distinct values, busiest `main` at 26,193. `cwd` has 83 distinct values but is not read: ccdrift already derives a project from the transcript path (`sessions.project_summary`), so `cwd` adds nothing the project dimension needs.
- **Prices are fitted, not guessed, and the fit checks itself.** As shipped in 0.12.x, before 0.13.0 fitted each model's cache-read rate (below): least squares against `costUSD ~ a*(input + 1.25*cache_write + 0.1*cache_read) + b*output + c*web_searches`, over the `cost-state` records on disk (116 when this price fit was run, 149 by a later recount): `claude-opus-4-7` fits at $5.00/$25.00 per Mtok in/out over 99 rows at 0.00% residual, `claude-sonnet-5` at $2.00/$10.00 over 12 rows at 0.00%, `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` at $1.00/$5.00 plus $10 per 1,000 web searches over 141 rows at 0.00%, `claude-opus-5[1m]` at about $5.25 over 37 rows at 0.47%, and `claude-opus-5` at about $5.16 over 8 rows at 0.34%. `claude-opus-5[1m]` prices as a key distinct from `claude-opus-5`, a distinction `message.model` itself does not carry.
- **The price join is exact string equality, and exact is the right join.** `ccdrift cost` matches a response's `message.model` against the `cost-state` key by `==`, the one place ccdrift matches a model name exactly rather than loosely. Measured over the owner's corpus: every one of 158,246 responses has a cost-state counterpart for its `message.model`, 100%, dated aliases such as `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` included, since `message.model` carries the dated form too. No normalisation step is needed, and adding one could only join a response to a price that is not its own. The divergence runs the other way and cannot be repaired from the response side: `claude-opus-5[1m]` appears among the cost-state keys and never in `message.model`. The fit does price the two keys apart, at the rates the entry above records for each, but nothing a response carries says which tier it ran on, so every 1m-context response prices at the plain `claude-opus-5` rate. That understatement is real and cannot be quantified from the responses at all, which is why the join carries a comment rather than a correction.
- **The cache multipliers are measured, not assumed.** 1.25x input for a cache write and 0.1x input for a cache read fall out of the two models whose four-parameter fit is exact, `claude-opus-4-7` and `claude-sonnet-5`. A free four-parameter fit is unusable and fails loudly rather than quietly: with the input and cache columns collinear over few rows, it returns a negative input price for `claude-opus-5` (-$10.09/Mtok) and `claude-opus-5[1m]` (-$24.38/Mtok). Constraining to the two exact models' ratios is what makes the fit well posed at all.
- **All-or-nothing bucket pricing was measured on the first real run and replaced.** `ccdrift cost` shipped in 0.12.0 pricing a bucket only when every model in it was priced, a rule the design never argued and the spec never stated. Run against the owner's corpus on 2026-09-22 over 2026-08-17..2026-09-21, 30 complete UTC days and 17,987,487,437 tokens, `claude-fable-5-1` carried 11 responses and 1,650,168 tokens, 0.0092% of the window, and left the top row of five of the six public dimensions with no dollar figure: subagent 60.5% of the window, `general-purpose` 59.3%, "no skill" 93.9%, "no plugin" 95.5%, "no MCP server" 94.5%. The window's total printed throughout, because 0.0092% clears `MATERIAL_SHARE = 0.01`. 0.12.1 applies that same cutoff to each bucket, against the bucket's own tokens rather than the window's, so a bucket is priced from what it can price while the rest stays under 1% and blanks above it. Of the eight buckets that held unpriced spend on that window, one per dimension, six now price and two do not: the `claude-fable-5-1` model row at 100% unpriced, and the `worktree-installable-check` branch at 1.82%, which is the evidence the cutoff discriminates rather than passing everything. A priced bucket keeps naming the models it could not price, so a `cost --json` row can now carry a non-null `dollars` beside a non-empty `unpriced` where before a figure implied an empty list: those dollars are a floor, and the line under the header gives the reason once. Nothing here alerts; it decides which figures print.
- **A branch row can name several repositories, and ccdrift counts folders rather than repositories.** Over the same window, 191 branch buckets. Collapsing each worktree folder to its repository root first, which is the analysis the design was argued from, 3 of those names spanned more than one repository and carried 18.0% of the window between them: `main` at 16.6% over five repositories splitting 11.7% + 3.1% + 1.5% + 0.2% + 0.1%, `HEAD` at 1.5% over two, and `review-fixes` at a rounding error. Qualifying the label as `project:branch` was written into the design and then measured out of it: ccdrift reads a project folder, which is a working directory, not a repository root, so one repository's `main` splits across its worktree folders (1.9% and 1.2% for this project), the ambiguous-name count rises from 3 to 5 on names shared only between two worktrees of one repository, and labels reach 93 characters in a 34-character column. 0.12.1 prints a count instead, on branch rows only, and it counts the unit ccdrift can actually see: project folders, not repositories. Both units appear above on purpose, because they give different numbers for the same rows and the shipped row uses the second: by project folder this window holds 5 pooled branch names over 16 folder-and-branch pairs, and the command prints `main` with 6 projects and `detached HEAD` with 4, against the 3 names and five repositories of the collapsed view. `HEAD`, which is what `git rev-parse --abbrev-ref HEAD` answers with no branch checked out, was 860 responses over those four project folders; it was already a bucket of its own before this release, and what changed is that it is now labelled `detached HEAD` rather than printing as though it were a branch name. The "no branch" bucket, which means the field was absent, held nothing in this window, consistent with `gitBranch` being on every response. A test written for that count exposed a smaller divergence in the same command, also fixed here: `cost --json` with no `--by` passed the JSON writer only the six default dimensions, so its `withheld` key came back empty and an omission the design calls named rather than silent was in fact silent; it now lists `project` and `branch`, with the dimensions themselves unchanged.
- **Models stopped sharing one cache-read ratio, so 0.13.0 fits each model's own.** The cache-multiplier bullet in this section measured 0.1x input for a cache read from two models that happened to agree. Claude Code 2.1.280 made `claude-opus-5-5` its default Opus model at $4.00/$20.00 per Mtok with cache reads at $0.20, 0.05x its input rate, and ccdrift's own field-arrival alert quoted that release note. Measured on the owner's corpus on 2026-09-23 over 406 cost records, refitting every priced model with the ratio fixed at 0.05x in place of 0.1x put all five over `MAX_RESIDUAL`, from `claude-sonnet-5` at 1.35% to `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` at 7.51%: the residual bound would have refused the new default model for good rather than misprice it, and once its share of spend passed 1% the window's total would have stopped printing. 0.13.0 keeps cache writes fixed at 1.25x, which a free fit recovers exactly on the three models whose free fit is well determined, and solves for the cache-read rate as a column of its own. That prices the same five models, recovers 0.100x on `claude-opus-4-7`, `claude-sonnet-5` and `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` and 0.099x and 0.090x on the two thinnest fits, `claude-opus-5` over 6 records and `claude-opus-5[1m]` over 24, and is never worse on residual: the worst admitted fit falls from 0.82% to 0.70%. Solving for every rate instead still returns negative input prices for those two, -$11.58 and -$15.05 per Mtok, which is why one ratio stays fixed. `MAX_RESIDUAL` keeps its value and now sits between that 0.70% and the nearest wrong-ratio fit at 1.35%, with the web-search ablation at 7.01%. The extra column costs a record: a model needs four cost records to be priced, five if it searched, where 0.12.1 needed three and four. A model whose records never read the cache is refused rather than charged nothing for its reads; none on this corpus is. On the owner's 30-day window the total moves from $10,463.41 to $10,463.09 as `claude-opus-5`'s rates are refitted, `claude-opus-5-5` stays unpriced at 0.63% of the window because it has written no cost record yet, and `cost --json` now shows each priced model's `cache_read_ratio`.
- **The per-model measurements behind that change.** On the same 406 records, every residual being the L1 figure `MAX_RESIDUAL` is checked against: fixing the cache-read ratio at 0.05x in place of 0.1x moved `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` (197 records, cache reads 46% of its billed tokens) from 0.00% to 7.51%, `claude-opus-4-7` (168, 33%) from 0.00% to 6.74%, `claude-opus-5` (6, 83%) from 0.63% to 1.72%, `claude-opus-5[1m]` (24, 87%) from 0.82% to 2.08% and `claude-sonnet-5` (10, 82%) from 0.00% to 1.35%. Solving for the read rate instead left `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, `claude-opus-4-7` and `claude-sonnet-5` at 0.00% with the ratio at 0.100x, `claude-opus-5` at 0.63% and 0.099x, and `claude-opus-5[1m]` at 0.70% and 0.090x, every rate positive. Leaving web search out of the solved fit misses `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` by 7.01% against 0.00% with it in; it searched in 4 of its 197 records. How many records carry the evidence differs sharply by model: every `claude-opus-5`, `claude-opus-5[1m]` and `claude-sonnet-5` record reads the cache, and all but one of `claude-opus-4-7`'s, while `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`'s reads, 46% of its billed tokens, come from 5 of its records. That is why the final review of 0.13.0 added one more refusal: a read rate that only one record sets is exactly determined by that record, so whatever its cost holds beyond its other counts is priced as reads at a residual of zero by construction. On a synthetic model with 10% of one reading record's cost unmodelled, the fit priced reads at 0.1218x against a true 0.1x. 0.13.0 refuses a model whose reads rest on a single record, reusing `MIN_EXTRA_ROWS` rather than adding a number; every model on this corpus clears it.
- **The fixed cache-write ratio is guarded only in part.** A model charging another write ratio is refused once the mismatch moves its residual past `MAX_RESIDUAL`, as cache reads fixed at 0.05x did on every priced model. Where writes are a small share of what its records cost, the fit can instead absorb the mismatch into its other rates within the bound: in the final review of 0.13.0 on 2026-09-23, synthetic records made at 2.0x and fitted at 1.25x priced at a 0.36% residual, billing each written token 39% under. No model on this corpus is known to charge other than 1.25x; the bound would not show it if one did.
- **The exact join met a model it could never price, so a model keyed only by its 1M tier is joined to that key.** The "exact is the right join" entry above held for every model on 2026-09-22. On 2026-09-24 `claude-opus-5-5`, Claude Code's default Opus model since 2.1.280, had 3,001 responses, all logged as `claude-opus-5-5`, and one cost-state record, keyed `claude-opus-5-5[1m]`, with no record under the plain name. Joined exactly, no response could ever meet a price, whatever the fit did. At 5.59% of the 30-day window it was past `MATERIAL_SHARE`, so `ccdrift cost` withheld the window's total, and as the default model its share only grows. The join now takes a model's `[1m]` key when its cost records carry no plain key at all. A model with both keys, `claude-opus-5` with 6 plain records and 25 `[1m]` ones, keeps its plain price, and one whose plain fit is refused stays unpriced rather than borrowing the 1M tier's: a response still can't say which tier it ran on. On that day this changes no figure yet. One record is under the fit's bar of four, with two reading the cache, so `claude-opus-5-5` stays unpriced and the total withheld until three more arrive. What the change removes is the case where that bar could never be met.

These counts come from a one-off measurement with no in-repo reproduction path, the same as the entry above says of its own: the feature ships no lab gate, and nothing in the repository re-derives them.

## Session-start components, 2026-09-24

What Claude Code logs about how a session starts, measured on 2026-09-24. The record counts below cover every transcript on disk that day; G14 reads complete days only, through 2026-09-23: 1,032 session starts, 46 of them CLI and the rest Agent SDK sessions (`sdk-py`).

- **What a session starts with is logged, most of it before the first response.** Nearly every transcript lists its skills, deferred tools and agent types before the first response. MCP instructions appear exactly in the 49 transcripts whose deferred tools include an MCP tool. CLAUDE.md files are logged from 2.1.263, and the system prompt from 2.1.267. The tool definitions come one record late: from 2.1.267 the snapshot Claude Code writes right after the first response carries them (575 transcripts, 14 built-in tools in all but one, about 83k characters), with a system prompt the same size as the snapshot before it in 573 of those. ccdrift now keeps the names and sizes of all of these, never their text or a CLAUDE.md path.
- **They can't be put in tokens yet (G14).** The gate fits each start's prompt tokens as a level per project, version, entrypoint and set of parts logged, plus a factor times the logged characters, and asks that the factor hold on random halves of the sessions and for each part on its own, within 20%. Over the 46 CLI starts the alert judges it fails outright: a pooled 0.129 tokens a character, halves from -0.261 to 0.472, and no part varied within its group in the 30 sessions a part needs (tool definitions 2, skills 20, CLAUDE.md 16, first message 29, the rest 17). Over all 1,032 starts, Agent SDK included (`--with-sdk`), the pooled factor is 0.421 (2.38 characters a token), every half within 20% (0.395 to 0.433), and CLAUDE.md (0.428 over 326 sessions) and the first message (0.422 over 991) agree with it. It still fails, because the parts a session-start alert would name barely vary within a group: tool definitions in 2 sessions, the skills listing in 20, the rest in 17. A factor measured on prompts and CLAUDE.md, at a density denser than prose, isn't one measured on a skills listing. So the session-start alert names what changed with its size in characters and makes no claim about its share of the token step. The 20% and the 30 sessions are judgements, as G3's bar is. What would answer it: sessions that differ in their skills, agents or tools within one project and version, which a machine that adds and removes them more often than this one would log. Reproduce with `python -m lab.components --today 2026-09-24`, and the wider fit with `--with-sdk`; both runs repeat byte for byte.
- **The one real step, read through the shipped comparison.** Over the sessions the detector found it in, 10 before 2026-08-27 and 3 from it (104k -> 128k), what Claude Code logged changed by 1 skill, 2 MCP tools and 1 deferred tool added, about 1.3k more characters, with CLAUDE.md, the system prompt and tool definitions not logged on those versions. The alert names that without claiming how much of the step it is, which G14 says the logs can't tell. The larger set measured for the spec (11 agent types, 4 skills and 11 MCP tools) compared 17 sessions before with 9 after, a wider window than the alert's. The kept change is the pooled pass's, not either project's own, but its window and baseline sessions all belong to the one project running at the time: comparing only within one project, as the alert does, changes nothing about this step.
